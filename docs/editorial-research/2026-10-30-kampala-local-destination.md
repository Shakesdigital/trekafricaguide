# Kampala local destination guide — 30 October 2026

## Scope and editorial choices

Selected Kampala as a capital-city guide for Uganda visitors who want time with Buganda heritage, faith and culture, food, and the city between longer road journeys. The guide treats Kampala as a working capital, suggests grouping stops by area, and does not present religious sites or markets as staged attractions. It offers two to three nights as editorial pacing, not a fixed tour itinerary.

The only current nearby published Uganda local guide is Buhoma, in southwestern Uganda. Other relevant published guides are for separate park regions (Bwindi, Kibale and Murchison Falls). None is linked as a nearby Kampala attraction or stay. Kampala has no published city attraction, activity, or accommodation record in this collection, so all relationship arrays are intentionally empty. Existing destination templates still provide the country destination card and search route from this Git-managed record.

## Claim checks

- Kampala is presented as Uganda’s capital; the Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA) tourism portal calls it “The Pearl of Africa’s Capital” and organizes city culture, shopping, food and attractions. Source: https://visitkampala.kcca.go.ug/
- Kasubi is described as a royal burial and cultural site. KCCA’s city portal gives its Kampala address and identifies its Buganda context; UNESCO’s World Heritage page is the authoritative property source: https://visitkampala.kcca.go.ug/business_details/kasubi-royal-tombs and https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1022/. Restoration and reopening reports were inconsistent, so the guide makes no guarantee of visitor access; Buganda Kingdom visitor information says sacred sites welcome respectful visitors, and readers are told to confirm current admission, hours, restoration status and ceremony-related access directly: https://buganda.or.ug/royal-tombs/.
- The Baha’i House of Worship and Kampala religious sites are drawn from the official KCCA portal’s religious-sites listing: https://visitkampala.kcca.go.ug/category_business/religious-site. The KCCA portal lists Namugongo Martyrs Shrine and its 3 June Uganda Martyrs Day Mass; the guide says to confirm the current programme and local access. Individual access is not promised; readers are directed to confirm access and avoid disrupting worship: https://visitkampala.kcca.go.ug/business_details/namugongo-martyrs-shrine.
- The official Uganda Museums and Monuments portal currently displays a temporary-closure notice for the Uganda National Museum. Although the same page contains general plan-your-visit hours and admission, those are not repeated because they conflict with the closure notice. Recheck opening directly before visiting: https://museums.tourism.go.ug/plan-your-visit/.
- KCCA’s official city portal has an Eat and Drink directory that names matooke and luwombo among traditional dishes. No restaurant endorsement, price or current availability is claimed: https://visitkampala.kcca.go.ug/category_business/eat-and-drink.
- Kampala–Entebbe Expressway is a four-lane tolled road linking Entebbe International Airport with Kampala, according to its official operator: https://kee.go.ug/. The guide gives no travel-time or toll-price estimate.
- For seasonality, the Ministry of Water and Environment’s September–December 2026 seasonal outlook describes two national rainy periods (March–May and September–December), notes regional differences, and forecasts near-normal to above-normal rainfall for much of Uganda. It is not a Kampala-specific daily forecast; the guide directs readers to check current local weather: https://wids.mwe.go.ug/.
- Safety and transport notes were checked against UK FCDO advice on 30 October 2026. The Kampala regional page gives city-specific context; the national safety page addresses demonstrations, valuables, travel after dark, matatus and boda-bodas. The guide identifies this as one government’s advice and points readers to their own government’s current guidance: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda/regional-risks and https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda/safety-and-security.
