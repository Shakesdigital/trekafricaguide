## Key facts checked 11 November 2026
- **UNESCO Medina of Tunis (ref 36, whc.unesco.org/en/list/36/, fetched)**: inscribed 1979; criteria (ii)(iii)(v); property 296.41 ha, buffer zone 190.19 ha; minor boundary modification 2010 (dossier 36bis). "Under the Almohads and the Hafsids, from the 12th to the 16th century, Tunis was considered one of the greatest and wealthiest cities in the Islamic world. Some 700 monuments, including palaces, mosques, mausoleums, madrasas and fountains, testify to this remarkable past." One of the first Arabo-Muslim towns of the Maghreb (698 A.D.); composed of the central medina (8th century) and suburbs to the North and South (13th century). Most remarkable monuments named: Zitouna Mosque, Kasbah Mosque, Youssef Dey Mosque, Bab Jedid Gate, Bab Bhar Gate, Souq el-Attarine, Dar el-Bey, Souqs ech-Chaouachia, Tourbet el Bey, Dar Hussein, Dar Ben Abdallah, Dar Lasram, Medrasa Es-Slimanya, El-Mouradia, El Attarine military barracks, Zaouia of Sidi Mehrez. National listing for 88 historic monuments, plus national protection for 5 monuments, 14 streets (including 3 souqs) and a square; protection via Law 35-1994 and the development plan of the Medina; safeguarding structure attached to the National Heritage Institute and a Safeguarding Association for the Medina attached to the Municipality of Tunis.
- **UNESCO Urban Heritage Atlas (whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/tunis, fetched)**: Tunis is "a large city of over one million inhabitants, expanding over a metropolitan area of 212 km2", in a fertile plain of north-eastern Tunisia a few kilometres from the sea; once a small Berber town (oppidum tunicense) linked to the Punic capital Carthage, developing significantly after the destruction of Carthage in the 8th century. Medina typology: walled with gates, courtyard buildings two to three stories high, narrow twisting streets, sabats bridging streets, minarets. Links list: **Tunis, UNESCO Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art (2017)**; harissa ICH 2022; couscous ICH 2020. Site-manager contribution (Montassar Jmour): during Ramadan the palaces and cultural centres of the medina open their doors for concerts, recitals and shows (Medina festival, Dream City at Beb Bhar Square) — carried as an attributed site-manager contribution.
- **UK FCDO Tunisia entry requirements (gov.uk, fetched 11 Nov 2026)**: for full 'British citizen' passports — passport valid for the duration of the stay; no visa up to 90 days (Crown Dependency passport holders may need a visa on arrival); authorities set and enforce entry rules; get your passport stamped; overstaying the 90-day period without a valid reason requires a fine; damaged/torn passports may cause difficulties leaving; British-Tunisian dual nationals should enter and leave Tunisia on the same passport. FCDO also advises against all travel to parts of Tunisia (warning line) — read current requirements rather than borrowing a rule.
