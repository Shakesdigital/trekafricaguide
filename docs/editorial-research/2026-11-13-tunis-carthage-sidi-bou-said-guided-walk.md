## Official Sources (all checked 2026-11-13)

### 1. ONMT (Office National du Tourisme Tunisien) — Discover Tunisia
**URL:** https://www.discovertunisia.com/en/discover/around-carthage-and-sidi-bou-said
**Key facts:**
- "Carthage is built on what is certainly one of the most beautiful sites of the Mediterranean... Discover fragments of history by accident in the streets of modern Carthage: the remains of the basilica of Saint Cyprien overlooking the sea, the Carthaginian houses of the Magon quarter, the Punic ports where a striking serenity reigns…"
- "Then climb the narrow paved streets of the famous neighbouring village, Sidi Bou Said, between blue mashrabiya latticework, pink bougainvillea and wrought iron filigree. Contemplate the stunning seascape from the Sidi Chabaane cafe, the lighthouse or the old cemetery."
- "Close by, La Marsa and its seafront, Gammarth and its long beaches both attract the locals of Tunis searching for a place to relax."
- Describes the area as "Coasts of Carthage, named after one of the most glorious ancient cities of the Mediterranean. Founded by the Phoenicians, destroyed then rebuilt by the Romans, Carthage is today a fashionable city."

### 2. AMVPPC (Agence de Mise en Valeur du Patrimoine et de la Promotion Culturelle) — Magon Quarter
**URL:** https://www.patrimoinedetunisie.com.tn/en/monuments/the-magon-quarter/overview/
**Key facts:**
- "An example of Carthaginian domestic architecture can be seen in the Magon Quarter, so-called because it was created at the time when the Magonids ruled Carthage."
- Residential area built from 5th century B.C. onwards on top of older landfills
- First dwellings modest, ~30 meters from rampart built along the shore
- Profound redevelopment from early 2nd century B.C.: large magnificently decorated houses (some >1,000 sq.m., up to 1,500 sq.m.) built from combination of older smaller houses
- Houses had underground cisterns, vaulted soakaways, stucco-covered columns and pilasters, painted walls, Punic mosaics (pavimenta punica)
- Houses accessed through narrow corridor to courtyard surrounded by porticoes
- Streets not paved, no drains

### 3. AMVPPC — Antonine Baths (checked via attraction page cross-reference; AMVPPC overview page returned 404 this run, but attraction page carries the paired-visit framing)
**Attestation:** Live attraction page explicitly pairs "Antonine Baths with the Magon Quarter" as a "focused two-stop Carthage visit": "Begin at the baths, the large Roman complex on Carthage's waterfront, then arrange a separate transfer to the Magon Quarter. The contrast helps make a broad archaeological landscape more legible: one stop focuses on Roman public architecture; the other preserves a Punic residential quarter, with houses and streets that offer a different view of life in ancient Carthage."
- Heritage agency lists a grouped ticket; attraction page advises: "confirm at the first ticket office whether the current ticket covers both places and whether either site has an access restriction that day. Posted hours vary by season and Ramadan, so check the current schedule before setting out."

### 4. TRANSTU (Société des Transports de Tunis) — TGM Suburban Railway
**URL:** https://www.transtu.tn/fr/faq (checked 2026-11-13)
**Key facts:**
- TGM stations listed: Tunis Marine, La Marsa (terminal stations for TGM line)
- TGM connects Tunis to coastal suburbs (La Marsa, Sidi Bou Said area)
- Ticket TGM (tarifs-ticket-tgm) exists as separate fare category
