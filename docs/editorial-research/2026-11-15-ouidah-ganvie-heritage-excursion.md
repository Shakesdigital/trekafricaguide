The live activities archive (HTTP 200, 15 November 2026) has 27 published activities and no Benin activity. The published Ouidah and Ganvié destination guide has an empty `activity_slugs` rail, while the attraction carries a combined GetYourGuide product and the nearby Casa del Papa stay.

The new activity guide should give readers an honest choice between a single guided combined day and separate, slower local outings. It must not imply that a boat stop, sacred site, ceremony or resident is automatically open to visitors, and it must not turn Ouidah's memorial history into a sightseeing checklist.

## Sources checked 15 November 2026

### Official Benin tourism

- [Bénin Tourisme: Ouidah](https://benintourisme.bj/villes/ouidah/) — official destination portal describes Ouidah as a Vodun spiritual centre and place of memory for the transatlantic slave trade; names Route des Captifs, Temple des Pythons and Forêt sacrée de Kpassè.
- [Bénin Tourisme: Ganvié](https://benintourisme.bj/destinations/ganvie/) — official destination portal describes Ganvié as a stilt village on Lake Nokoué, founded between the 17th and 18th centuries by Tofin communities; fishing, trade, floating market and water-based daily life. Practical information: access through the Abomey-Calavi embarkation point; motorised pirogue or paddled crossing; lifejackets supplied and required throughout; recommended visit 2–3 hours; paid entry on site; ask permission before photographing residents.
- [Bénin Tourisme: La Route des Captifs](https://benintourisme.bj/inspirations/la-route-des-captifs/) — official page says the route is about 4 km, links Ouidah's historic centre to the beach, begins at the Portuguese fort and passes Place aux Enchères, Arbre de l'Oubli, Case Zomaï, Mémorial de Zoungbodji, Porte du Non-Retour and Bateau de la Mémoire. It says the contemporary memorial route was reconstructed in the 1990s under UNESCO's Slave Route project and substantially upgraded in the 2020s. It recommends walking when possible, a guide, water, a hat and suitable shoes; free access overall, but some spaces or guided visits may charge; suggested duration 2–4 hours.
- [Bénin Tourisme: Forêt sacrée de Kpassè](https://benintourisme.bj/inspirations/la-foret-sacree-de-kpasse/) — official page describes the sacred forest as an active spiritual place, with Vodun sculptures and the oral tradition of King Kpassè and the sacred iroko. Practical information: daily access, suggested 30–45 minutes, 1,000 FCFA entry, guided visit, reception/ticket area, toilets and parking; some areas may close on celebration days.
- [Bénin Tourisme: Ouidah practical/travel pages](https://benintourisme.bj/villes/ouidah/) — official portal's practical navigation points to visa, access, health and safety, currency, timing and etiquette pages; use these for the current official framing rather than inventing details.

### Official travel advice and entry

- [UK FCDO: Benin travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin) — current at 15 November 2026; check the current regional advisory before extending beyond southern Benin. Southern Benin, including Ouidah and Ganvié, is not the part of the country covered by the northern/eastern advisory.
- [UK FCDO: Benin entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/benin/entry-requirements) — passport valid for the duration of stay; visa required; apply online at least four working days before arrival; yellow fever certificate required. The FCDO page says travellers cannot obtain Nigeria, Ghana or Togo visas in Benin.
- [Benin e-Visa portal](https://evisa.bj/) — official online visa application route named by the FCDO. Confirm nationality, passport, transit and visa category before booking; a tour operator does not grant entry.

## Editorial position

Publish an activity guide for a **Cotonou-based guided heritage day** with two clear variants: the exact 7-hour combined product for readers who want one operator-managed day, and a slower two-part local plan for readers who want separate Ouidah and Ganvié outings. The guide will not claim that the combined product is suitable for every traveller or that every site is open. It will make the memorial context explicit and avoid presenting Vodun practice or residents' homes as spectacle.
